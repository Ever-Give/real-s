University and Holding On

Author: SuperKaylee

*000*2024-01-06 02:54

It is no secret that I am awful at maintaining control of my bodily functions. Why this is the case I have no idea. As far as I was aware growing up I was normal... well, as normal as could be for someone severely accident prone. Fast forwarding 10 years and that child is now an adult living hundreds of miles away from their home and family. It's an unnerving experience for sure but most likely a common one, be it students or those simply wanting to move on with their lives and independency. I've always been the family loving cry baby, but in September I made the bold step to leave the safety of my home and embark on my quest for a degree.

Finding a new home is a challenge for anyone to overcome. These experiences typically came with positives and negatives. For me, this effect was doubly so as now I had a brand new home where nobody knew me. I've always been a people pleaser but I do acknowledge that I can overthink a lot. The problem in this case was that although I was free of my reputation at my true home, I was now hyper analysing everything and praying not to ruin this second chance. As of this writing I am happy to report I'm still going strong at almost 4 months since my last 'incident'. I'm sure you all know what I'm referring to by 'incident'. While I am thrilled with 4 months progress, let me assure you that is has not been easy. On the very starting day I met my first of hopefully many allies. Compared to my 5' 2" nature they were 40 square feet... pleasure to meet you, en-suite bathroom. 

While I immediately fell in love with this new friend the downside was that they were limited to remaining stationary right next to where I slept. A couple of days later the fates nearly evened the scores. Some afternoon while adventuring around campus I had nearly destroyed the record that was going almost a week strong at this point. The culprit was obvious; my blatant coffee addiction teaming up with unfamiliar environment and challenging me to a duel. Fortunately for me lady luck was on my side, saving me from severe 'hands-shoved-into-trousers' syndrome. Claiming the win I gloat to my non-existent opponent "not today!"... I mean, sure, I've had a few close calls but victories are victories all the same.

Within the last 4 months a new adversary has appeared that is neither friend nor foe, lecture-theo-ter (🤪). Theo is lovely in that I get to experience my love for learning history in a supporting environment of like minded peers, but also infuriating in that it feels awkward having to silently excuse myself away from them to tend to my needs in the middle of a speech or some form of presentation. I've already lost count the amount of times I've had to leave and then return later on. In hindsight, I suppose it's not Theo's fault that I bring drinks to every single lecture I have. Perhaps I'm lacking the willpower needed to give myself a wake-up slap and yell "function without coffee just this once!". I'm not sure what it is about the drink, but between that and orange or apple juice it does not take long for my body to interrupt my activities with a polite 'ahem'.

In the interim, a technique I have been slowly trying to master is the art of holding. This ability is like a sacred martial art in that it is not easy to replicate for someone who can get panicked by a twig snapping. Given my track record, fears and my still-developing feelings for this scene it's been tough, slow progress. On the deliberate attempts of training (particularly recently) I can recall becoming tense, developing dread at the 'what if' and struggling to get to grips at what points and timings I start to physically claw at myself. It's not like a clock countdown appears in the right hand side of my vision with "Attempt started: ETA 5 minutes until dam burst", though that would make it a lot easier to practice. I have wondered if between my anxiety and the fact I am intentionally trying to force this is what is making it so bad?

Subconsciously practicing the technique has yielded mixed data. By this, I mean being in situations where I instinctively start holding myself without naturally thinking or planning it out. So far this has happened in two specific scenarios. This originally happened in the heart of my dorm while my 2 roommates were making a group meal for the whole group (6 of us a communal kitchen, but our rooms are private). I don't know why I neglected to listen to my body and just say a polite be right back but the final results were gradually wiggling sat on the chair while my body became heavier, insides sloshing side to side and an occasional burning kept lifting its head. Was I in a trance? What was wrong with me? Thankfully I came to my senses without a comment from either of my dorm friends and hastily hurried to my room to empty my internal bottle. You know it's a good time when you audibly sigh.

This becomes a bit more fearful when you come to the realization that you're holding in your pee whilst in the middle of a lecture. Why? Haven't a clue. All I can say for sure is that earlier this week while happily taking notes on ancient coinage and their uses beyond being a currency, the Costa I had brought with me decided to magically duplicate. Sure, it was a 3 hour lecture, but that's split into segments for breaks as otherwise you would experience a swift burnout. 

How do you go from having 100% health to suddenly bursting at the seams?! Bodily distractions and grabbing at my arms soon indicated I was in trouble. At first I tried to hold off. Looking at the clock showed me not long was left. The smart brainwaves finally triggered and I hurried out to my freedom. Public bathroom, but in really good condition, which probably spared me from ruining my win streak. My only embarrassment was missing the last 5-10 minutes and waiting for the slides to appear online later to fill in the blanks.

Another wall of text, more food for thought but on the brighter side its general musings and no shame this time! It's still 'weird' not knowing how I feel about it overall, but still we progress. Thanks for the love!
